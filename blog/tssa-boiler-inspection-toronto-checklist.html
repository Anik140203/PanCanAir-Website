Why TSSA inspections exist in Ontario

The Technical Standards and Safety Authority (TSSA) is the independent administrator delegated by the Ontario government to enforce Ontario Regulation 220/01 — the Boilers and Pressure Vessels Regulation under the Technical Standards and Safety Act, 2000.

For commercial boilers in Toronto (essentially any boiler in a multi-residential or commercial high-rise), this means:

  • Annual inspection by a TSSA-licensed boiler inspector O. Reg. 220/01 s.10
  • Maintenance of a complete service logbook O. Reg. 220/01 s.14
  • Operating permit displayed on or near the boiler at all times O. Reg. 220/01 s.9
  • Repairs and modifications only by TSSA-registered contractors O. Reg. 220/01 s.16

A failed inspection in Ontario isn't a paperwork issue — TSSA has statutory authority to order immediate shutdown of non-compliant boilers, which during a Toronto winter is a building-wide crisis.

The 14 items inspectors fail buildings on most often

Across the 80+ TSSA inspections we attend each year with PanCanAir clients, these are the items that generate non-compliance notices most frequently — in order of how often we see them written up:

01Incomplete logbookMissing monthly low-water cutoff tests or water treatment readings. The single most common failure — over 40% of inspections we attend.
02Expired water treatment chemistrypH out of range or hardness/conductivity outside manufacturer spec. Almost always preventable with a $200/month treatment program.

10Permit displayed in wrong locationMust be near the boiler, not in the building office. Surprisingly common.
11Safety valve discharge piping issuesRestricted, undersized, or improperly routed discharge piping.

The document binder you need on-site

When the inspector arrives, you should be able to put the following in their hands within 60 seconds. Keep a physical binder in the mechanical room, even if you also have digital copies.

  • Current Certificate of Inspection (last year's)
  • Operating permit (currently displayed)
  • Boiler logbook — complete past 12 months
  • Water treatment records — monthly chemistry analysis
  • Pressure relief valve test certificate — annual
  • Combustion analysis report — annual
  • Service contract with TSSA-registered contractor
  • All repair/modification records with R-stamp documentation
  • Manufacturer operating manual
  • Operator certification (Class 4 if applicable)

What to do in the 30 days before inspection

  1. Day 30: Confirm renewal date with TSSA. Review past inspection report for unresolved items.
  2. Day 25: Have your contractor do a full pre-inspection walk. Resolve outstanding items in writing.
  3. Day 20: Run a combustion analysis if not done in the past 12 months. Update the logbook.
  4. Day 15: Test the pressure relief valve. Document and file the certificate.
  5. Day 10: Update water treatment chemistry records. Confirm consumables (chemicals, filters) are stocked.
  6. Day 5: Walk through with your contractor. Practice the "on-arrival" handover — having the binder, logbook, and current readings ready in 60 seconds.
  7. Day of: Mechanical room cleaned. Combustibles removed. All paperwork on a table near the boilers.

If you fail

You'll receive a written Inspection Report identifying each non-compliance item with one of three severity codes:

  • Notice: Minor item, typically 30–90 days to correct. Operating permit renewed.
  • Order: Significant item, typically 7–30 days to correct. Re-inspection required.
  • Immediate Order: Safety-critical item. Operating permit suspended. Boiler must be shut down until corrected.

For any Order or Immediate Order, contact a TSSA-registered contractor immediately. Document the correction work, then submit a written response to TSSA with photos and the corrective work invoice. Most non-emergency Orders are clearable within 14 days if you respond quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often does TSSA inspect commercial boilers in Ontario?

TSSA-registered commercial boilers in Ontario require annual inspection by a TSSA-licensed boiler inspector. The exact frequency depends on the boiler's category (steam vs. hot water, pressure rating, BTU input). Most condo boilers are categorized 'low-pressure hot water' and inspected annually.

What documents does TSSA require on-site during inspection?

You need: current Certificate of Inspection posted near the boiler, complete logbook of service activity for the past 12 months, water treatment records, pressure relief valve test records (annual), low-water cutoff test records (monthly), and any modification or repair records signed by the licensed contractor who performed them.

What happens if a boiler fails TSSA inspection?

Outcomes depend on severity. Minor non-compliance items generate a written notice with a typical 30–90 day correction window. Major items can result in immediate operating-permit suspension, meaning the boiler must be shut down until corrected. Repeated non-compliance can result in administrative monetary penalties.

Does TSSA inspection apply to small residential boilers?

In Ontario, boilers under 250 BTU/hr input are typically exempt from TSSA registration. However, virtually all commercial high-rise boilers exceed this threshold and are subject to TSSA registration and annual inspection under the Boilers and Pressure Vessels Regulation (O. Reg. 220/01).
