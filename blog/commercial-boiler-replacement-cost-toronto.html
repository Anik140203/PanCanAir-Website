Commercial boiler replacement cost in Toronto: the 2026 guide.

By Pankaj Oberoi·Owner & HVAC Engineer·Oct 21, 2026·11 min read

The boiler is the heart of a GTA building's heating plant — and replacing it is the capital decision most likely to arrive as an emergency instead of a plan. Here's what commercial boiler replacement actually costs in Toronto in 2026, when to replace versus repair, and how to make sure you're never doing it in January with residents in parkas.

Commercial boiler replacement cost by type and size

Boiler replacement cost swings widely based on three things: the boiler type (cast-iron sectional vs. condensing), the capacity, and how hard it is to get the old unit out and the new one in. These are realistic 2026 installed prices across GTA buildings:

Boiler category (per unit)2026 installed cost
Cast-iron sectional, 500K–1M BTU$28,000 – $48,000
Condensing gas, 500K–1M BTU$38,000 – $62,000
Cast-iron sectional, 2 MMBTU$42,000 – $65,000
Condensing gas, 2 MMBTU$52,000 – $85,000
Modular condensing bank (4 × 500K)$95,000 – $145,000

These installed prices include basic controls integration, standard near-boiler piping, and removal of the old unit. They do not automatically include the three cost drivers below, which is where quotes diverge most.

The three line items that blow up a boiler quote

1. Rigging and access

Getting a two-ton cast-iron boiler out of a basement mechanical room — up a freight elevator or through a tight penthouse — can rival the cost of the equipment itself. Add 15–30% for difficult access. Always have each bidder state their rigging plan and cost explicitly; a suspiciously low quote often assumes easy access it hasn't verified.

2. Venting

Condensing boilers run cooler flue gas and need Category IV stainless-steel venting. Many older GTA buildings have terracotta-lined chimneys that can't be used as-is. If your building needs a new vent system, that's a real added cost — and occasionally the reason a building stays with a (less efficient) non-condensing boiler.

3. Controls integration

If your building automation system is pre-2010, integrating a modern boiler's controls may require a BAS upgrade. A good quote states whether controls integration to your existing system is included or extra.

Repair or replace? The decision framework

The single most common mistake we see is replacing a boiler that could have been repaired — or nursing one that should have been replaced years ago. Use this framework:

Replace when…Repair when…
Past 20–25 years oldUnder 15 years old
Cracked or leaking heat exchangerHeat exchanger sound
Efficiency dropping, fuel climbingFailure is a replaceable part
Single repair > ~40% of replacement costRepair is a fraction of replacement

A mid-life boiler with a failed circulator pump or control board is almost always worth repairing. An end-of-life boiler with a cracked heat exchanger is not — a cracked exchanger is both a reliability and a safety issue, and patching it just delays the inevitable at higher total cost.

Condensing vs. cast-iron: the payback math

When you do replace, the big choice is condensing (high-efficiency) versus conventional cast-iron. Condensing costs 30–45% more upfront and delivers 15–25% fuel savings over a 15–20 year life.

THE MATH

On a building spending $150,000 a year on gas, a 20% efficiency gain is $30,000 a year. Even a $25,000 upfront premium for condensing pays back in well under two years of fuel savings — and keeps saving for another 18. Unless your venting situation makes condensing impractical, it's usually the clear economic choice.

The real cost nobody quotes: emergency replacement

Every number above assumes a planned replacement. An emergency mid-winter replacement is a different and worse story: premium pricing, longer timelines because the right boiler may not be in stock, temporary rental boilers, and residents without heat while it all happens. The emergency version routinely costs 25–50% more and comes with a furious board.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to replace a commercial boiler in Toronto?

In 2026: roughly $28,000–$48,000 for a cast-iron sectional (500K–1M BTU), $38,000–$62,000 for a condensing boiler of the same size, and $95,000–$145,000 for a modular condensing bank. Add 15–30% for difficult access. Prices include basic controls and old-unit removal.

Should I repair or replace a commercial boiler?

Replace if it's past 20–25 years, the heat exchanger is cracked, efficiency is falling, or a single repair exceeds ~40% of replacement cost. Repair if it's under 15 years, the exchanger is sound, and the failure is a replaceable part.

Are condensing boilers worth the extra cost?

Usually yes — 30–45% more upfront for 15–25% fuel savings over 15–20 years, typically paying back in 4–6 years (often faster on large gas bills). The exception is buildings whose venting can't accommodate condensing without a costly upgrade.

How long does a commercial boiler replacement take?

A planned replacement takes 3–10 days depending on access, rigging, and venting/controls upgrades. Schedule in spring or fall to minimize heat disruption. Emergency mid-winter replacements cost more and take longer — which is why planning it into the reserve fund is far cheaper.
