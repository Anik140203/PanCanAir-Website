Fan coil unit maintenance for Toronto condos: the complete 2026 guide.

By Pankaj Oberoi·Owner & HVAC Engineer·Oct 15, 2026·11 min read

The fan coil unit is the most-ignored and most-dangerous piece of equipment in a GTA condo. It lives inside the suite, out of sight, quietly growing mold in its drain pan — until it overflows into the unit below and becomes a $9,000 water-damage claim. Here's how property managers keep every fan coil in the building out of that headline.

What a fan coil unit actually does — and why it fails

A fan coil unit (FCU) is the small heating-and-cooling box inside each suite — usually hidden in a closet, above the entry, or under a window. Hot or chilled water from the building's central plant runs through its coil; a fan blows suite air across that coil to heat or cool the unit. It's simple, reliable, and almost universally neglected because nobody sees it.

Every fan coil fails in one of a small number of predictable ways. Knowing them tells you exactly what a maintenance visit must cover:

  • Clogged condensate drain pan — the number one failure. The cooling coil produces condensation that drips into a pan and out a drain line. Dust plus moisture grows biofilm, the drain clogs, water backs up and overflows. This is the leading cause of in-suite water damage in the GTA.
  • Dirty coil — a dust-caked coil can't transfer heat. The suite won't cool properly, the fan runs longer, and the central plant works harder — raising the whole building's energy bill.
  • Seized or noisy fan motor — bearings wear, the motor gets loud, then stops. Residents call it in as "my AC died."
  • Stuck zone valve or actuator — the valve that controls water flow fails open or closed, so the suite is stuck hot, stuck cold, or won't respond to the thermostat.
  • Clogged filter — restricts airflow, strains the motor, and drops comfort. The cheapest thing to fix and the most frequently skipped.
THE ONE THAT COSTS YOU

Four out of five in-suite water-damage claims we're called to in GTA condos trace back to a clogged fan coil drain pan. A $15 condensate treatment tablet and a 10-minute pan flush, twice a year, prevents nearly all of them. Skipping fan coil maintenance to save money is the most expensive saving in building management.

The fan coil maintenance schedule that actually protects the building

For a typical GTA high-rise, here's the cadence we run — built around the two things that cause claims (drain pans) and complaints (filters):

TaskFrequency
Filter replacement2–4× / year
Condensate drain pan clean + treatment tablet2× / year
Condensate line flush2× / year
Coil cleaning1× / year
Fan motor & bearing inspection1× / year
Zone valve / actuator test1× / year
Thermostat calibration check1× / year

The two pre-season visits are the backbone: one in April–May before cooling (the drain pan matters most when the coil is producing condensation) and one in September–October before heating. Buildings that run only one visit a year almost always run it in spring and leave the heating-season check undone — which is why zone-valve-stuck-cold complaints spike every November.

The hard part isn't the work — it's the access

Here is what nobody tells new property managers: fan coil maintenance is 20% mechanical skill and 80% logistics. The unit is inside someone's home. Getting a technician into all 240 suites is the real job.

What separates a smooth fan coil program from a miserable one:

  1. Notice period. Ontario requires reasonable notice for entry (typically 24 hours written). Build the notice into a template and send it 5–7 days out, with a specific date and a 4-hour window per floor.
  2. Floor-by-floor batching. A technician should clear an entire floor in a session, not bounce between floors. Schedule by stack.
  3. No-access tracking. 10–20% of suites will miss the first pass. You need a clean list of who was missed and a scheduled second sweep — not a sticky note that gets lost.
  4. Resident communication. The complaint isn't the maintenance; it's the surprise. Tell residents what's happening, why it protects them, and what they need to do (clear the closet, secure pets).
WHERE SOFTWARE PAYS FOR ITSELF

The no-access list is where fan coil programs quietly fail — suites get missed, nobody tracks it, and those are the exact units that leak in August. A proper mechanical dashboard logs every suite's service date, photo, and no-access status, so the second sweep is automatic and the board can see 100% completion before AGM season.

Fan coil maintenance and the aging-building problem

Most GTA condos built in the 2005–2015 boom are now 11–21 years old — exactly the window when original fan coils start reaching end of life. If your building is in that range, two things should be on your radar:

Rising repair frequency. When you start seeing more than roughly 8–10% of units needing a repair (not just maintenance) per year, you're entering the replacement curve. That's the signal to start a reserve-fund line for phased fan coil replacement rather than replacing reactively, one failure at a time.

Refrigerant and parts obsolescence. Some older fan coil models are getting hard to source parts for. A good maintenance partner flags which models in your building are approaching parts obsolescence so replacement can be planned, not forced during a July heat wave.

A simple fan coil checklist for your next maintenance cycle

  1. Confirm two visits per year are contracted — spring (pre-cooling) and fall (pre-heating).
  2. Confirm drain pan cleaning + condensate treatment is explicitly in scope, not an add-on.
  3. Require a per-suite completion log with no-access tracking and a scheduled second sweep.
  4. Require photo documentation of each drain pan — your proof of due diligence if a claim ever arises.
  5. Ask your contractor to flag units entering the repair curve so you can plan reserve-fund replacement.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should fan coil units be serviced in a condo?

At least twice a year — before cooling season (April–May) and before heating season (September–October) — with filters changed 2–4 times a year. The service must include drain pan cleaning, condensate treatment, and a line flush, which is what prevents in-suite water damage.

Why does my condo fan coil smell musty or leak water?

Almost always a clogged condensate drain pan or line. Water backs up, overflows, and grows mold. It's the most common fan coil failure and the leading cause of in-suite water claims. Regular pan cleaning and a treatment tablet prevent it.

How much does fan coil maintenance cost per unit in Toronto?

A twice-yearly program runs $90–$180 per unit per year in 2026. A repair is $220–$650; a full replacement is $1,200–$2,400 installed. Preventive maintenance is far cheaper than the water damage one clogged drain pan causes.

Who is responsible for fan coil maintenance — the corporation or the owner?

In most Ontario condos the corporation schedules and funds building-wide fan coil maintenance even though the unit sits inside the suite; owners provide access. Always confirm against your specific declaration, as the split varies.
